Challenge

Our sales team, spread across locations, tracked estimates in a shared spreadsheet and worked the deals themselves out of the inbox — two places that never talked to each other. Follow-ups slipped, nobody could see what actually needed attention today, and managers had no daily picture of the pipeline.

Solution

Rather than move everyone onto a CRM that would fight the way we already worked, we built our own around it. I designed and built DealBoard Command, a Chrome extension that puts the CRM inside Gmail as a sidebar, synced live to our Google Sheets pipeline through Apps Script. Every open estimate becomes a card you act on in one click, follow-up emails drop straight into Gmail compose, and a daily dashboard tells each rep their next move. Nothing gets copied between the sheet and the inbox any more, nothing slips through the cracks, and the day goes to the deals that are actually worth the time — without anyone leaving the inbox they already live in.

Results

  1. No copying between the spreadsheet and the inbox: the sidebar writes to the pipeline as the rep works.
  2. Delivered 100% pipeline visibility and structure with a KPI dashboard and pipeline tracking system, eliminating leaks and lead stagnation.
  3. One-click follow-up, approve, cancel and reschedule on every estimate, without leaving the inbox.
  4. Rescheduled deals return to the top of the board on the chosen day, so nothing is ever forgotten.
  5. Follow-up email templates injected directly into Gmail compose.
  6. A daily "Today" view: calls and emails due, win, conversion and cancellation rates, and a recommended next move.
  7. Live pipeline stats: active, approved and canceled value, follow-up depth and win rate.
  8. An automated end-of-day sales summary email for each rep.
  9. A cutting-edge, luxury interface, built to feel like a premium product rather than an internal tool.

Challenge

Logic Raw's clients were founders of B2B firms who knew exactly what their business should do and very little about what their brand needed to say. Briefs arrived as ambitions rather than requirements, and the design and development teams could not build from them. Without someone owning that translation, work stalled in the gap between what a client meant and what a team could execute — and the client paid for the delay.

Impact

I was the client's single point of contact and the team's brief. I ran discovery with founders to pin down positioning, audience and the commercial goal behind the work, then turned that into messaging and a go-to-market strategy the design and development teams could actually build against. I stayed on each account through delivery and past it — reviewing output against what the client needed rather than what had been asked for, managing expectations in both directions, and keeping the relationship intact once the project shipped. Where it moved the client's numbers, I worked alongside the founders on the identity and messaging myself.

Results

  1. Led 20 client accounts as the single point of contact, from first brief through to post-launch support.
  2. Translated founder goals into briefs the design and development teams could build from, and reviewed the work back against them.
  3. Worked with founders on positioning, messaging and go-to-market, lifting client conversion rates by 35%.
  4. Automated 75% of acquisition with CRM and campaign tooling, and ran the multi-channel campaigns on top of it.
